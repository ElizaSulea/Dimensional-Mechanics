This series of papers proposes a bold new idea: that the universe as we know it (its shape, quantum behavior, and even time) emerges from a kind of geometric filtering process, not from fundamental particles or randomness. Instead of assuming space and time just "exist," the model treats them as byproducts of recursive collapse through higher dimensions. Imagine space as the literal third dimension, a sphere zoomed all the way out, where curvature creates a surface tension. This tension, when calculated, perfectly matches the mysterious cosmological constant that drives the universe’s expansion, without needing any vacuum energy or dark energy particles. It's like reinterpreting the universe’s behavior as the result of geometric balance, not invisible forces.

As the papers build, they introduce quantum-like behavior as interference patterns between waves in these extra dimensions. Rather than randomness, “quantum weirdness” is shown to emerge from structured resonance between fields in layered geometry. Finally, the series proposes a filtering process governed by spin and parity, that collapses unstable higher dimensions down to just the three we experience. Dimension 3 survives not by chance, but because it's the only one stable under this recursive filtering. Time, in this view, isn’t a thing that ticks forward, but the index of how many layers a structure survives through. What looks like quantum probability, cosmic acceleration, and even dimensionality itself may all be echoes of deeper recursive geometry. The universe may yet be a machine collapsing all dimensions and we may be in a narrow survivability zone.
